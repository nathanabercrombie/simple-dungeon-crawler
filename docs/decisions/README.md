# Decision Log

This file records settled choices separately from ideas still under discussion. New decisions should include a date, rationale, influences, and consequences for later design.

## 2026-03-31 — Initial project constraints

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Design an original TTRPG that is lethal, supports solo play as a first-class mode, and makes extensive use of procedural tables. Build a lightweight local web app to support procedural generation and potentially other play functions.

**Rationale:** These constraints define the intended play experience and keep the rules and software efforts pointed at the same goal.

**Consequences:**

- Every major rules subsystem must be evaluated for both group and solo use.
- Lethality must arise from understandable procedures rather than arbitrary surprise alone.
- Procedural content is part of the game design, not supplemental filler.
- Core play must remain possible without the web app.
- Mechanics influenced by other games must be attributed in the inspiration notes and expressed in original language.

## Decision states

- **Proposed:** under active consideration.
- **Accepted:** currently part of the design.
- **Superseded:** replaced by a later decision, with a link to its replacement.
- **Rejected:** considered and deliberately not adopted.

## 2026-03-31 — Design the core loop first

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Scope the core play loop before selecting the action-resolution system or implementing a web generator.

**Rationale:** The repeated activities and decisions of play should determine what resolution mechanics, solo support, procedural tables, and software are actually needed.

**Consequences:** The current expedition loop is documented as a proposal. Its individual steps are not accepted rules until reviewed.

## 2026-03-31 — Support lone adventurers and parties equally

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** One player may control either one adventurer or a small party, and both configurations receive equal rules support.

**Rationale:** The game should accommodate an intimate solo-character experience and the tactical and survival dynamics of party play without treating either as a variant bolted onto the other.

**Consequences:** Encounter pressure, action economy, resource consumption, and procedural generation must scale with adventurer count. Multi-character play must remain light enough for one player to administer.

## 2026-03-31 — Adopt a room-by-room free-form exploration loop

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Exploration advances by entering and generating rooms or areas, interpreting their contents, and taking free-form actions. Contents can include monsters, traps, treasures, puzzles, clues, environmental features, or empty space. A GM or oracle establishes uncertain facts and responses.

**Influences:** *Four Against Darkness*, *2D6 Dungeon*, *Ironsworn: Delve*, and traditional dungeon exploration associated with *Dungeons & Dragons*.

**Rationale:** This combines strong procedural structure between locations with open-ended problem solving inside them.

**Consequences:** Room generation must produce interactive situations rather than only combat encounters. The oracle needs to answer questions generated by free-form play. The resolution rules should be invoked by risk and uncertainty rather than by a fixed menu of moves.

## 2026-03-31 — Explore separate light and alert pressures

**Status:** Superseded

**Decision:** Investigate a predictable light countdown inspired by *Shadowdark* alongside a reactive dungeon-alert mechanism inspired by *Clank!*.

**Rationale:** The two systems can create different forms of tension: dwindling safe time and consequences caused by noisy, greedy, or prolonged intrusion.

**Open question:** Determine whether alert uses a token bag, dice pool, cards, or a track, and decide whether the light countdown uses real time or exploration turns.

## 2026-03-31 — Measure light in exploration turns

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Light sources count down in exploration turns rather than real time. Certain adverse roll outcomes may consume additional turns.

**Influence:** *Shadowdark* provides the desired urgency and importance of light, but the implementation uses game time so solo and party play face comparable pressure.

**Consequences:** The room procedure must define when an exploration turn passes. Time loss becomes one possible cost for a dangerous roll, and the eventual light durations must be balanced against room generation and encounter frequency.

## 2026-03-31 — Prototype a single-d20 duality roll

**Status:** Rejected

**Decision:** Test a single d20 whose modified total determines success while its natural parity determines disposition: even indicates Hope and odd indicates Fear.

**Influence:** This adapts the two-axis outcome texture of *Daggerheart* to the desired tactile experience of rolling one d20.

**Rejection:** The two-axis result and possible resource economy add more complexity than desired. The game will instead use success or failure to control whose turn it is.

## 2026-03-31 — Let success and failure control turn ownership

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Use a single d20 resolution roll. On a success, it remains the adventurers' turn. On a failure, the intended action does not succeed, any result inherent to the fiction occurs, and control passes to the DM or dungeon. In solo play, the oracle-driven dungeon procedure takes that role.

**Influence:** Consequence-oriented, fluid turn flow associated with *Daggerheart*, simplified into a binary handoff without Hope/Fear dice or metacurrencies.

**Rationale:** Turn ownership gives success and failure an immediate, easily understood effect while preserving the tactile appeal of one d20.

**Consequences:** The handoff is the default systemic cost of failure; failure does not automatically add an unrelated penalty before the dungeon acts. The rules must prevent large parties from gaining unlimited actions merely by choosing the best adventurer for each successive roll.

## 2026-03-31 — Give the dungeon one meaningful action

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Each handoff gives the dungeon one meaningful action with a monster, coordinated monster group, hazard, or environmental event. Control then returns to the adventurers.

**Rationale:** A single action keeps handoffs fast and makes failure dangerous without allowing every enemy in a crowded room to overwhelm a lone adventurer at once.

**Consequences:** A group or swarm may act as one threat. Later combat rules must define group actions and calibrate their impact. The GM and solo procedure need clear guidance for choosing the most fictionally appropriate dungeon action.

## 2026-03-31 — Share adventurer-side turn ownership

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Whenever the adventurers control the turn, any adventurer may act next, including the adventurer who most recently acted. Do not require rotation or track which adventurers have acted during a round.

**Rationale:** Shared turn ownership keeps party operation as fluid as controlling one adventurer and avoids adding a separate initiative or action-tracking system.

**Consequences:** Fictional position determines who can act and prevents impossible interventions. Players may repeatedly use the best-positioned adventurer, so later character design should favor distinct situational strengths rather than enforcing spotlight through turn order.

## 2026-03-31 — Spend a turn when entering a new room

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Entering and generating a new room consumes one exploration turn. Lengthy activities such as thorough searching, resting, or involved trap work consume additional turns.

**Rationale:** Room entry provides a consistent heartbeat for light depletion, while additional costs let time respond to meaningful choices inside the room.

**Consequences:** Light duration can be expressed in rooms of baseline progress. Movement through known areas and exact costs for lengthy activities remain to be defined. Certain adverse rolls may also consume extra turns, as previously accepted.

## 2026-03-31 — Grant Skill Tokens for conquering rooms

**Status:** Proposed

**Decision:** After a room is conquered, give every active adventurer one Skill Token. Merely entering or re-entering a room grants nothing, and each room can grant its reward only once.

**Possible use:** Tokens may activate or strengthen special abilities.

**Rationale:** Entering a room consumes light and exposes the adventurers to danger; earning power only after overcoming the room rewards completion rather than door-opening.

**Open question:** Define concrete special-ability effect benchmarks within the accepted cost range.

## 2026-03-31 — Define room conquest by durable resolution

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** A room is conquered when its main challenge is durably resolved. Defeating enemies, negotiation, puzzle solving, trap neutralization, and a durable clever bypass can all qualify. Fleeing or merely postponing the challenge does not.

**Rationale:** The reward should recognize completion while preserving free-form problem solving and avoiding an incentive to kill every creature.

**Consequences:** Room generation needs to make the main challenge identifiable. Empty or safe rooms do not automatically produce tokens; they need a meaningful generated challenge to become eligible.

## 2026-03-31 — Give each adventurer a personal Skill Token

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** When a room is conquered, every active adventurer gains one personal Skill Token.

**Rationale:** Token gain should follow characters rather than the number of humans at the table. A party then uses the same resource economy whether controlled by one solo player or several players.

**Consequences:** Every adventurer needs a small personal token track or pool. Party size increases both the number of tokens gained and the number of characters whose abilities those tokens may need to power.

## 2026-03-31 — Make Skill Tokens expendable

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Skill Tokens are consumable. When an adventurer uses a token-powered effect, the Skill Token is removed from that adventurer's personal pool.

**Rationale:** Spending creates choices between using power now and conserving it for later danger. It also lets room conquest replenish capability without making power increase permanently during an expedition.

**Consequences:** Abilities and other eligible effects need explicit token costs. A capacity and starting amount remain to be chosen.

## 2026-03-31 — Clear Skill Tokens after an expedition

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Every adventurer loses all unspent Skill Tokens when the expedition ends.

**Rationale:** Tokens represent momentum generated by overcoming the current dungeon. Clearing them prevents characters from farming easy rooms and hoarding power for later expeditions.

**Consequences:** Players are encouraged to spend tokens before extracting, while retreat still preserves persistent rewards such as treasure and survival. The rules must define the exact boundary at which an expedition ends.

## 2026-03-31 — Require tokens to activate special abilities

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Adventurer special abilities require expenditure of Skill Tokens. They do not have free versions that tokens merely enhance.

**Rationale:** Conquering rooms should directly unlock discrete moments of exceptional character capability and create meaningful decisions about when to spend earned power.

**Consequences:** An adventurer at zero tokens cannot activate special abilities but retains ordinary free-form actions. Ability design must specify token costs and remain useful enough to justify saving a scarce, temporary resource.

## 2026-03-31 — Start each expedition with one Skill Token

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** At the beginning of every expedition, each active adventurer receives one fresh Skill Token.

**Rationale:** Every adventurer should be able to activate at least one special ability before conquering the first room.

**Consequences:** The opening room can assume access to one Skill Token expenditure per adventurer. Skill Tokens from a previous expedition are still discarded; the starting token is a new allowance, not retained currency.

## 2026-03-31 — Cap Skill Tokens at three per adventurer

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** An adventurer can hold no more than three Skill Tokens. Skill Tokens gained beyond the cap are lost and cannot be transferred.

**Rationale:** A low cap keeps physical tracking simple and encourages players to use special abilities instead of hoarding power throughout the expedition.

**Consequences:** Special abilities can naturally use costs from one to three tokens. Conquering a room while at capacity yields no token benefit for that adventurer, making timely spending valuable.

## 2026-03-31 — Use roll-high d20 checks

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** For an uncertain, consequential action, roll `d20 + bonus` and meet or exceed a target number. Use the relevant attribute bonus unless a skill applies.

**Influence:** Roll-high d20 resolution associated with *Dungeons & Dragons* and related games.

**Rationale:** A single d20 is tactile, familiar, and quick to interpret. Target numbers allow a GM or solo procedure to express difficulty directly.

**Consequences:** The target-number scale and numerical ranges for attributes and skills must be calibrated against the failure-driven dungeon turns.

## 2026-03-31 — Print attributes inside total skill bonuses

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** A skill's character-sheet bonus already includes its linked attribute. Roll `d20 + listed skill bonus`; do not add the attribute again. If no skill applies, add the relevant attribute alone.

**Rationale:** Ready-to-roll totals reduce calculation and make a multi-adventurer party easier for one person to run.

**Consequences:** Whenever an attribute or skill rank changes, derived skill totals must be updated. Character sheets and the web app should make the inclusion of the attribute visually clear.

## 2026-03-31 — Use 2d20 advantage and disadvantage

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** With advantage, roll 2d20 and keep the higher natural die. With disadvantage, roll 2d20 and keep the lower natural die. Add the applicable bonus after choosing the die.

**Influence:** Advantage and disadvantage from *Dungeons & Dragons* 5e.

**Rationale:** This represents favorable or unfavorable circumstances without changing arithmetic and preserves the tactile appeal of d20 rolls.

**Consequences:** Circumstances can substantially change probability without adding arithmetic.

## 2026-03-31 — Do not stack advantage or disadvantage

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Advantage and disadvantage are binary states. Multiple sources of the same state do not add dice. If at least one source of each applies, all advantage and disadvantage cancel and the character rolls 1d20.

**Influence:** The cancellation and non-stacking convention used by *Dungeons & Dragons* 5e.

**Rationale:** Players need only determine whether advantage, disadvantage, both, or neither applies; they never count or compare situational sources.

**Consequences:** One minor disadvantage can cancel several advantages and vice versa. Exceptional circumstances that should transcend this rule will need explicit mechanics other than stacking extra d20s.

## 2026-03-31 — Use a four-step target-number ladder

**Status:** Accepted for initial playtesting

**Decision:** Use target numbers 9 (Easy), 12 (Standard), 15 (Hard), and 18 (Extreme). Standard 12 is the default when no procedure specifies a difficulty.

**Influence:** The compact difficulty-class ladder from *Shadowdark*.

**Rationale:** Four values are easy for a GM to remember, straightforward for procedural content to reference, and sufficient to distinguish meaningful levels of difficulty without false precision.

**Consequences:** Solo tables and room elements can name a difficulty category instead of requiring ad hoc target selection. The ladder must be probability-tested after attribute and skill ranges are established.

## 2026-03-31 — Use three broad attributes

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Use Strength, Agility, and Mind. Mind includes both Wits-like functions—perception, reasoning, and knowledge—and Will-like functions—resolve, presence, and supernatural discipline.

**Influences:** Compact three-attribute structures in *Maze Rats* and *Mausritter*.

**Rationale:** Three attributes keep character management fast for solo-controlled parties. Folding Wits and Will together avoids splitting mental actions across two overlapping statistics.

**Consequences:** The combined Mind attribute is broad, so linked skills must create meaningful specialization among intellectual, perceptive, social, and magical characters.

## 2026-03-31 — Finalize the attribute names

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Name the three attributes **Strength**, **Agility**, and **Mind**. Strength replaces the earlier working name Might; Mind is confirmed as the name of the combined Wits/Will attribute.

**Rationale:** The names are direct, familiar, and broad enough to communicate each attribute's scope without requiring setting-specific terminology.

**Consequences:** Skills, character sheets, procedural checks, and future rules should use these names consistently.

## 2026-03-31 — Record only attribute bonuses

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Attributes are direct bonuses added to d20 checks. Do not use a separate score or a score-to-modifier conversion.

**Influence:** This deliberately streamlines the score-plus-modifier convention used by traditional *Dungeons & Dragons* while retaining roll-high d20 checks.

**Rationale:** Only the modifier affects the core check. Printing that value directly removes an unnecessary lookup and makes solo party management faster.

**Consequences:** Character creation, advancement, and effects must refer directly to attribute bonuses. A value such as Strength +2 is complete and has no hidden corresponding score.

## 2026-03-31 — Use a polarized starting attribute array

**Status:** Accepted for initial playtesting

**Decision:** Starting adventurers assign +3, +0, and −2 among Strength, Agility, and Mind.

**Rationale:** A wide spread creates more exciting and dynamic characters with a defining strength and a consequential weakness.

**Consequences:** Before skills or advantage, success against Standard 12 is 60%, 45%, and 35% respectively. Party members have clear niches. To support a lone adventurer equally, room design and free-form adjudication should permit alternate approaches rather than repeatedly mandating one attribute.

## 2026-03-31 — Give each adventurer two free-form skills

**Status:** Superseded by the Past-and-Knack model

**Decision:** Each starting adventurer creates two short, free-form skill phrases. Each skill is linked to one attribute, grants +2 when clearly relevant, and is recorded as a total that already includes the linked attribute. Invoking a skill costs one Skill Token, spent before the roll.

**Influence:** Player-authored Experiences from *Daggerheart*.

**Rationale:** Free-form phrases communicate character history and competence without requiring a long universal skill list. Two skills create distinction while keeping a solo-controlled party concise.

**Consequences:** A skill linked to a +3 attribute is printed as +5, one linked to +0 as +2, and one linked to −2 as +0. Without spending a Skill Token, the adventurer rolls the underlying attribute. The rules need guidance and examples for judging relevance, preventing overly broad phrases, and later improvement.

## 2026-03-31 — Do not stack free-form skills

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** No more than one free-form skill can modify a roll. If several phrases are relevant, choose one. Advantage and disadvantage remain separate and can apply normally.

**Rationale:** Non-stacking skills prevent broadly worded experiences from combining into oversized bonuses and keep each check quick to assemble.

**Consequences:** Spending additional Skill Tokens cannot add a second skill to the same roll unless a future ability explicitly overrides this rule.

## 2026-03-31 — Name the resource Skill Tokens

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** The personal resource earned by conquering rooms and spent on free-form skills or special abilities is called a **Skill Token**.

**Rationale:** The name foregrounds the resource's role in invoking character expertise.

**Consequences:** “Conquest token,” “room token,” and “momentum” are retired working terms. Skill Tokens remain usable for both free-form skills and special abilities unless a later decision splits those resources.

## 2026-03-31 — Price stronger abilities with more Skill Tokens

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Special abilities cost from one to three Skill Tokens. Stronger abilities have higher costs.

**Rationale:** Variable costs let the three-token pool express a clear power scale and create a choice between several modest effects and one major effect.

**Consequences:** One-token abilities should be routine special techniques, two-token abilities should have substantial impact, and three-token abilities should be signature, encounter-changing, or emergency effects. Ability design must establish more precise balance benchmarks.

## 2026-03-31 — Use natural 20 and natural 1 critical results

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** A natural 20 on the retained d20 is an automatic critical success with an added benefit. A natural 1 is an automatic failure, advances the exploration clock by one additional turn, and hands control to the dungeon as a normal failure does.

**Influences:** Critical faces from d20 games and light-clock pressure inspired by *Shadowdark*.

**Rationale:** The d20's extreme faces should create memorable tactile moments. Making natural 1 consume time implements the earlier goal that certain bad rolls deplete light.

**Consequences:** Natural 1 is an explicit exception to the rule against adding a generic extra penalty to failure. It does not give the dungeon more than its normal one meaningful action. Under advantage or disadvantage, discarded dice cannot trigger critical effects. Previously spent Skill Tokens are not refunded by a critical result unless a specific rule says otherwise.

## 2026-03-31 — Define harm before attack procedures

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Design HP, lasting injury, and death before deciding whether enemy attacks roll to hit or deal damage automatically.

**Rationale:** The harm model determines how lethal and consequential a dungeon action is, so attack procedures cannot be evaluated in isolation.

**Consequences:** Combat attack design is paused while the Hit Protection model is scoped.

## 2026-03-31 — Use renewable Hit Protection and lasting Scars (superseded)

**Status:** Proposed

**Decision:** HP represents Hit Protection and replenishes after a fight genuinely ends. At 0 HP, an adventurer is exposed; a hit while exposed causes a Scar and reduces an attribute bonus by 1.

**Influences:** The Hit Protection and post-HP attribute harm lineage of *Into the Odd*, *Cairn*, and *Mausritter*.

**Rationale:** Renewable HP makes each fight readable, while Scars and attribute loss carry lethal pressure across the expedition and campaign.

**Retained portion:** HP is Hit Protection; a hit that reduces it to 0 causes no lasting harm and excess damage does not spill over. The later-hit rule is replaced by the Wound-to-Scar model below.

**Open questions:** Determine which attribute is reduced, how repeated hits work, the precise boundary that restores HP, and how incapacity, death, and recovery function.

## 2026-03-31 — Give exposed adventurers one-hit warning

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Damage stops at 0 HP and never spills over. A hit that reduces HP to 0 does not cause lasting harm. Only a subsequent hit received while already at 0 HP causes a Wound.

**Rationale:** Reaching 0 HP should clearly signal immediate danger and give the adventurer or party one opportunity to retreat, defend, or intervene before a lasting injury occurs.

**Consequences:** Large damage cannot bypass the warning state. Damage amount matters while HP remains, but any hit capable of reaching an exposed adventurer causes a source-specific Wound unless a later rule distinguishes attacks.

## 2026-03-31 — Convert acute Wounds into lasting Scars

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** A hit against an adventurer already at 0 HP causes a source-specific Wound. One active Wound imposes disadvantage on every check plus its own effect. Receiving a second active Wound causes immediate death. Appropriate treatment converts the Wound into a Scar, removes its acute effects, and reduces the attribute specified by that Wound by 1.

**Influences:** Hit Protection and attribute harm from the *Into the Odd*, *Cairn*, and *Mausritter* lineage, expanded with monster-specific acute and lasting injury states.

**Rationale:** Wounds make enemy attacks distinct and create an urgent treatment problem, while Scars preserve long-term consequences without leaving every injured adventurer at universal disadvantage.

**Consequences:** Monsters and damaging hazards need Wound entries defining an acute effect and the attribute reduced after treatment. Treatment must be possible for a lone adventurer but costly under pressure. HP amount, damage scale, treatment procedure, and Scar recovery remain open.

## 2026-03-31 — Compare two HP scales

**Status:** Superseded

**Decision:** Playtest either approximately 5 HP with ordinary d6 damage or 2 HP with ordinary attacks dealing 1 damage.

**Rationale:** Both aim for roughly two to three successful enemy hits before the first Wound, but one emphasizes variable tactile damage while the other emphasizes deterministic speed.

**Consequences:** The chosen scale will determine how much design space exists for armor, heavy attacks, weak attacks, healing, and HP differences between adventurers.

## 2026-03-31 — Give adventurers 2 HP and use 1 ordinary damage

**Status:** Accepted for initial playtesting

**Decision:** Adventurers have 2 HP. An ordinary monster hit deals 1 damage. Damage stops at 0, so three ordinary hits without HP recovery cause the first Wound.

**Rationale:** The sequence is fast, predictable, and legible: first hit weakens protection, second exposes, third wounds. Fixed damage keeps attention on source-specific Wounds.

**Consequences:** HP offers very little numerical granularity. Armor, exceptional attacks, and defensive abilities must be designed carefully because a one-point change is substantial.

## 2026-03-31 — Use low monster HP and fixed player damage

**Status:** Accepted for initial playtesting

**Decision:** A successful adventurer attack normally deals 1 damage. Monsters use simple HP bands: 1 fragile, 2 standard, 3 tough, and 5 boss-tier. At 0 HP, a monster is defeated according to intent and fiction. A natural 20 may use its added benefit to deal +1 damage.

**Influences:** Roll-high attacks and Defense targets from d20 games, combined with low numerical tracking from minimalist dungeon crawlers. This deliberately does not copy the automatic-hit attack procedure of the *Into the Odd* lineage.

**Rationale:** Monster HP directly communicates the number of successful attacks required, while fixed damage avoids a second roll after every d20 check.

**Consequences:** Weapons need differentiation through tags, attack conditions, or explicit damage exceptions. Groups and bosses need procedures that avoid excessive HP inflation.

## 2026-03-31 — Give every weapon a distinct benefit

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Successful ordinary weapon attacks deal 1 damage, while every weapon provides a distinctive benefit such as range, defense, conditional advantage, control, concealment, group utility, or bonus damage against a narrow vulnerability.

**Influences:** Fiction-first equipment in minimalist dungeon games and creature-specific damage interactions from fantasy adventure games.

**Rationale:** Weapon choice should change tactics and fictional options without requiring a large table of damage dice.

**Consequences:** Because monster HP is low and attack failure grants a dungeon turn, broad extra damage and unconditional advantage are premium effects. Weapon benefits need strong conditions or tradeoffs, and no weapon should dominate all situations.

## 2026-03-31 — Prototype an initial weapon roster

**Status:** Proposed

**Decision:** Test signature benefits including sword parrying for +1 maximum HP, conditional advantage for dual daggers, bow range, spear reach, axe cleave, maul disorientation and extra damage against Brittle creatures, mace armor penetration, risky two-damage great weapons, and whip control.

**Rationale:** A varied prototype roster will reveal which types of benefit remain useful under the fluid turn-handoff system.

**Consequences:** Range, engagement, armor, Brittle creatures, weapon swapping, and control effects need precise definitions before the roster can be finalized.

## 2026-03-31 — Build character creation from linked random tables

**Status:** Proposed

**Decision:** Generate an adventurer through a few correlated rolls. The first roll supplies signature gear and sets its associated attribute to +3. A second roll, keyed to that result, supplies a negative trait and defensive package and sets one of the remaining attributes to −2. The final attribute becomes +0.

**Influence:** *Cairn* first edition's random gear generation and especially second edition's thematic backgrounds with two small character-defining tables.

**Rationale:** Linked results make stats and gear reinforce one another while retaining the surprise and speed of procedural generation.

**Consequences:** The first table needs an even distribution of Strength, Agility, and Mind signature gear. The second must never assign −2 to the +3 attribute. Armor mechanics and a player choice or reroll valve remain open.

## 2026-03-31 — Treat magical implements as signature gear

**Status:** Proposed

**Decision:** The character-creation table can provide a spellbook, wand, sacred relic, alchemical satchel, rune focus, or familiar-command implement in place of a conventional weapon. Such gear sets Mind to +3, enables a basic 1-damage attack, and seeds token-powered special abilities.

**Rationale:** Mind-focused characters need a coherent route into the same combat and equipment procedure as weapon users.

**Consequences:** Each implement needs a distinctive benefit comparable to a weapon benefit, plus clear rules for range, magical attacks, and granted abilities.

## 2026-03-31 — Prototype Past and Knack skill tables

**Status:** Partially superseded by the layered ability proposal below

**Decision:** Generate the two starting free-form skills from separate d20 prompt tables. Past describes a bounded former life; Knack describes a specific repeatable talent. After rolling, the player permanently links each phrase to one attribute and explains its meaning through that attribute.

**Influences:** Character-defining sub-tables in *Cairn* second-edition backgrounds and player-authored Experiences in *Daggerheart*.

**Rationale:** One broad background phrase and one precise talent create complementary hooks without becoming a universal skill list.

**Consequences:** The phrase applies only when both its fictional scope and linked attribute fit. The draft tables need playtesting for results that are too broad, too narrow, redundant, or difficult to adjudicate solo.

## 2026-03-31 — Separate gear techniques, Past, and Knack

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Give character features three distinct sources. Signature gear provides a free basic function and may grant token-powered techniques. Past is one free-form +2 skill costing 1 Skill Token. Knack is one concrete personal special ability costing 2 Skill Tokens.

**Influences:** Resource-gated Experiences and abilities from *Daggerheart*, combined with the equipment-defines-capability approach of *Cairn*.

**Rationale:** Each source then has a clear purpose: gear establishes tactics, Past provides flexible expertise, and Knack guarantees one distinctive personal capability.

**Consequences:** This replaces the two-free-form-skill model with one free-form skill and one defined ability. Knack prompts need mechanical effects rather than only evocative phrases.

## 2026-03-31 — Distinguish spellbooks from magical weapons

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** A spellbook contains token-powered spells but provides no basic attack. A wand is a Mind-based ranged weapon or focus that makes a basic 1-damage attack and may also carry token-powered powers.

**Rationale:** Books communicate learned abilities, while wands occupy the same immediate combat role as bows and melee weapons.

**Consequences:** A spellbook user needs a separate attack implement. Character-generation packages may bundle a spellbook with a wand, staff, dagger, or other basic weapon.

## 2026-03-31 — Prototype tiered gear abilities

**Status:** Accepted for initial playtesting

**Decision:** Equipment may have a free basic benefit plus techniques costing one to three Skill Tokens. Examples include Whip Disarm for 1, Greatsword Whirlwind for 2, and the spell Cure Wounds for 3.

**Rationale:** Tiered costs turn signature gear into a meaningful source of abilities without converting every ordinary action into a special move.

**Consequences:** Not every mundane weapon needs a complete three-tier tree. Higher-tier techniques can come from exceptional gear, advancement, or discoveries. Cure Wounds removes one active Wound without a Scar and restores full HP, so its three-token cost consumes a full pool.

## 2026-03-31 — Draft an oversized d66 signature-gear catalog

**Status:** Proposed material for reduction

**Decision:** Use a 36-entry candidate catalog—12 Strength, 12 Agility, and 12 Mind packages—to explore signature-gear identities before selecting a smaller final table.

**Influences:** d66 procedural tables from old-school and solo role-playing games, fiction-first equipment from *Cairn*, and token-powered techniques from the project's adopted ability model.

**Rationale:** Deliberate overproduction makes it easier to compare overlapping designs, identify the desired setting tone, and cut weak or redundant entries.

**Consequences:** None of the individual entries are accepted merely by appearing in the catalog. The final table should retain equal attribute representation and must be checked for solo viability, unconditional advantage, excess damage, and effects that erase dungeon actions.

## 2026-03-31 — Draft keyed Burden and Armor tables

**Status:** Proposed material for reduction

**Decision:** Draft three d12 tables keyed to the character's +3 attribute. Each contains six packages for either possible −2 attribute and supplies a Burden, protection, candidate benefit, and fictional constraint.

**Influences:** Thematic equipment packages from *Cairn* and correlated random character generation developed for this project.

**Rationale:** The keyed tables guarantee a valid +3/+0/−2 array while making weakness and equipment imply a coherent history.

**Consequences:** The armor vocabulary—Reinforced, Guard, Resist, Insulated, and Stable—is exploratory. Flat damage reduction is excluded because it would negate ordinary 1-damage attacks. Stacking with weapon defenses and the final distribution of mundane versus supernatural armor remain open.

## 2026-03-31 — Draft concrete Knacks next

**Status:** Accepted as the next design task

**Decision:** Complete the character-generation components by drafting an oversized catalog of concrete personal Knacks, each normally costing 2 Skill Tokens, before reducing the existing candidate tables.

**Rationale:** Signature gear and Burden/Armor generate stats and equipment, and Past already has prompts. Knacks are the missing component needed to generate complete playable adventurers for testing.

**Consequences:** The catalog should cover exploration, survival, social interaction, defense, offense, occult problems, and solo-oracle support. Knacks should not merely duplicate equipment techniques.

## 2026-03-31 — Draft an oversized d66 Knack catalog

**Status:** Proposed material for reduction

**Decision:** Draft 36 concrete Knacks costing 2 Skill Tokens across exploration, survival, social, defensive, offensive, and occult/oracle categories.

**Influences:** The project's *Daggerheart*-influenced resource economy, *Cairn*-like concrete capability, and *Ironsworn*-style solo information tools.

**Rationale:** A broad catalog enables later cutting and provides the final missing component for generating complete sample adventurers.

## 2026-03-31 — Move next to room procedural generation

**Status:** Accepted as the next design focus

**Decision:** Pause character-creation reduction after drafting Knacks and define the room-by-room dungeon-generation procedure.

**Rationale:** Room generation is the central activity loop and will reveal what character features, equipment, oracle tools, and the web app must support.

**Consequences:** The first required choice is whether generated sites use literal floorplans, abstract node maps, or hybrid sketch maps.

## 2026-03-31 — Generate literal dungeon floorplans

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Procedural dungeon generation creates an exact floorplan with room shapes, corridors, doors, and relative placement on a square grid rather than an abstract node map or loose sketch.

**Influences:** Emergent mapping in *Four Against Darkness* and *2D6 Dungeon*.

**Rationale:** Literal mapping makes spatial exploration, secret rooms, loops, tactical positioning, and the physical growth of the dungeon central pleasures of play.

**Consequences:** The tabletop and web procedures need deterministic placement, rotation, collision, loop, boundary, and vertical-connection rules. The next choice is whether room footprints come from templates or rolled dimensions.

## 2026-03-31 — Generate rooms from authored templates

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Generate exact room footprints from a template table. Templates may be rotated or mirrored when legal and include authored internal terrain such as pillars, pits, balconies, galleries, bridges, pools, stairs, rubble, and raised platforms.

**Influences:** Room-by-room mapping in *Four Against Darkness* and *2D6 Dungeon*.

**Rationale:** Templates are quick to reproduce on a grid while producing more memorable and tactically meaningful geometry than independently rolled rectangular dimensions.

**Consequences:** Each template needs entry anchors, exit sockets, legal orientations, terrain cells, and collision behavior. The catalog must be large enough to avoid repetitive sites.

## 2026-03-31 — Escalate through an active d20 room roster

**Status:** Superseded by sliding d20 room escalation

**Decision:** Begin with master room templates 1–20 assigned to d20 faces. After a template is rolled and used, cross it off and replace that face with the next locked master template, beginning with 21. Used rooms never repeat within the site, and the active roster always contains twenty entries.

**Influence:** Increasing delve danger associated with *One Deck Dungeon*, adapted into a persistent draw-without-replacement room pool.

**Rationale:** The procedure uses the same satisfying d20 for every room, gradually introduces harder results, preserves occasional early rooms, and avoids rerolling crossed-off entries.

**Consequences:** The master list must be ordered along a danger curve. Later templates need danger tags that affect monsters, hazards, or other challenges without making every deep room a combat encounter. Paper play needs a twenty-slot roster; the web app can update it automatically.

## 2026-03-31 — Use room Danger Rank as the default target

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Each room template has a Danger Rank of Easy 9, Standard 12, Hard 15, or Extreme 18. That number is the default Defense or target for enemies, traps, hazards, puzzle mechanisms, and other opposed obstacles in the room unless a specific entry overrides it.

**Rationale:** One room-level value makes escalation immediately affect every kind of challenge and reuses the core target-number ladder.

**Consequences:** Individual threats need numerical overrides only when their identity demands it. Danger Rank affects resistance to adventurer actions but does not determine monster HP, damage, Wound effects, or room content by itself.

## 2026-03-31 — Separate room geometry from room contents

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** A room template specifies its exact geometry, internal terrain, tags, and Danger Rank but does not prescribe whether the room contains a monster, trap, puzzle, social obstacle, treasure, or another challenge. Site-themed tables generate those contents separately.

**Rationale:** Separating footprint from content gives each authored template greater replay value and lets the same terrain support different situations across sites.

**Consequences:** Content generation must interpret terrain tags so pillars, pits, balconies, and other features materially affect the generated challenge rather than remaining decorative.

## 2026-03-31 — Test sliding d20 room escalation

**Status:** Accepted for initial playtesting

**Decision:** Generate room-template indices with `d20 + (E × N)`, where N is rooms already generated and E is an Escalation value from 1 to 3. Cross off used templates and reroll duplicate results. The rising minimum retires low-numbered unvisited rooms.

**Influence:** Escalating delve difficulty associated with *One Deck Dungeon*, combined with draw-without-replacement procedural tables.

**Rationale:** Unlike the active roster, the formula prevents very easy rooms from remaining available indefinitely. The multiplier provides a transparent difficulty and pacing dial.

**Consequences:** E 1 produces slow escalation, E 2 is the default, and E 3 produces fast escalation. Individual sites may override the default. The master catalog must be long enough for the maximum expected modifier, and duplicate reroll frequency needs physical-table testing.

## 2026-03-31 — Default site Escalation to 2

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Site Escalation E defaults to 2. A site may specify E 1 for a long gradual delve or E 3 for a short, rapidly intensifying delve.

**Rationale:** A default keeps ordinary generation immediate, while site-specific overrides turn escalation speed into a tool for expressing dungeon character and expected length.

**Consequences:** At the tenth generated room, the default template range is 19–38. Site descriptions and generators need to display any nonstandard Escalation value clearly.

## 2026-03-31 — Define sites with Domain and Theme

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Every dungeon site has one Domain describing its original identity or function and one Theme describing its current condition or transformation. Roll them for an unknown site or select them when established by the fiction.

**Influence:** Theme and Domain site construction from *Ironsworn: Delve*.

**Rationale:** Combining two reusable vocabularies gives generated rooms coherence without requiring a unique table for every dungeon.

**Consequences:** Domain tables must supply room functions and structural motifs. Theme tables must transform those functions through conditions, inhabitants, hazards, and sensory details. Room content generation must combine them into actionable situations rather than outputting two unrelated prompts.

## 2026-03-31 — Use modular Domain and Theme cards

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Do not write a bespoke content table for every Domain × Theme pairing. Each Domain and Theme receives a compact reusable card, and the universal Challenge and Opportunity procedure combines fields from the active pair only as needed.

**Influence:** Modular Theme and Domain composition from *Ironsworn: Delve*, adapted to literal rooms and multi-axis content generation.

**Rationale:** Modular cards prevent combinatorial table explosion while allowing any Domain and Theme to interact.

**Consequences:** Card fields need a consistent schema. Pair-specific pages may demonstrate combinations but are examples, not required rules modules.

## 2026-03-31 — Build Haunted Tomb as the first vertical slice

**Status:** Accepted as a prototype

**Decision:** Test the modular architecture with a Tomb Domain card, Haunted Theme card, and a worked Haunted Tomb example containing challenge interpretations, opportunities, sample threats, Wounds, and generated rooms.

**Rationale:** One complete combination can expose missing fields and interpretation burden before more modules are written.

**Consequences:** The slice is test material rather than final setting canon. Its results should be evaluated for specificity, solo usability, repetition, and speed.

## 2026-03-31 — Let the GM adjudicate template collisions

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Do not create a random collision-resolution subsystem. The GM adjusts room orientation, mirroring, connector length, placement, shared walls, or the rolled result when a template overlaps existing geometry. A solo player makes the least disruptive good-faith adjustment or rerolls.

**Rationale:** Human judgment can preserve a plausible map more quickly than a detailed collision algorithm.

**Consequences:** The web app should flag overlaps and offer manual controls rather than resolving them invisibly. Exact collision behavior is not required for the tabletop core.

## 2026-03-31 — Give most rooms multiple onward exits

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Most room templates provide at least two visible onward exits in addition to the entry used to place the room. Intentional dead ends remain possible.

**Rationale:** Multiple exits create branching route choices, loops, missed areas, and meaningful retreat planning.

**Consequences:** The first twenty templates are revised so nineteen provide at least two onward routes when visible vertical connections are counted; the dead-end shrine is the exception. A site-size procedure must prevent the frontier from expanding forever.

## 2026-03-31 — Test a bounded macro-grid of room sectors

**Status:** Accepted for initial playtesting

**Decision:** Place room templates inside a larger finite grid of sectors, with at most one room per sector. Exits lead toward adjacent sectors; entering an empty connected sector generates a room, while reaching an occupied sector can form a loop.

**Influence:** The bounded branching topology of *Clank!*, combined with this project's literal room templates.

**Rationale:** A macro-grid caps site size, supports frequent branching and loops, and avoids room overlap without abandoning exact internal floorplans.

**Consequences:** Size packages are 3×3 with E 3, 4×4 with E 2, and 5×5 with E 1. Each sector is an 11×11 canvas supporting a room footprint up to 9×9 plus a one-square connector margin. Multi-sector rooms, boundary exits, and objective placement need later rules.

## 2026-03-31 — Pair site size with escalation speed

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Compact 3×3 sites use E 3, standard 4×4 sites use E 2, and sprawling 5×5 sites use E 1 unless a specific site overrides the pairing.

**Rationale:** Short sites need to reach dangerous templates quickly, while larger sites need slower escalation. Pairing size and speed gives each format a comparable danger arc.

**Consequences:** Site generation can present size as one choice that establishes both maximum room count and default escalation. Overrides remain possible for unusually gentle or brutal sites.

## 2026-03-31 — Prototype a far-edge Heart and Revelations

**Decision:** Test a hybrid objective with a Heart placed on the far edge of the macro-grid and several Revelation markers hidden in other sectors. Revelations integrate into normal Opportunities and remove specific layers from the final threat. They make the intended resolution safer but do not absolutely prevent an early confrontation.

**Influences:** Deep objectives and extraction from *Clank!* plus progress-through-discovery ideas from *Ironsworn: Delve*.

**Rationale:** The Heart makes spatial depth meaningful, while Revelations reward branching and prevent a lucky short route from trivializing the whole site.

**Consequences:** The Haunted Tomb prototype places three Revelations, requires two for its intended Hard resolution, and grants an improved Standard resolution and relic for finding all three. ## 2026-03-31 — Generate Challenge and Opportunity independently

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Roll room Challenge and Opportunity as separate axes. Challenge determines the room's main problem and conquest condition. Opportunity determines an additional clue, supply, useful feature, treasure, secret, shortcut, major discovery, or absence of extra reward.

**Influences:** Combinatorial random tables from *Maze Rats* and room-by-room content procedures from *Four Against Darkness* and *2D6 Dungeon*.

**Rationale:** Independent rolls allow useful combinations such as monster plus treasure, trap plus shortcut, or quiet room plus clue instead of making content categories mutually exclusive.

**Consequences:** The Opportunity should be integrated into or placed in tension with the Challenge. Quiet rooms do not automatically have a conquest condition or award Skill Tokens.

## 2026-03-31 — Risk danger and light while traveling through conquered rooms

**Status:** Accepted for initial playtesting

**Decision:** Spend one exploration turn to travel through up to three connected conquered rooms. Roll a d6 upon entering each conquered room; on a 1, it becomes dangerous again and movement stops. Reaching an unconquered or already dangerous room also stops movement. Apply this procedure during deeper travel, backtracking, and extraction.

Conquest does not erase persistent traversal features. Crossing a pit, difficult climb, deep water, unstable rubble, narrow ledge, or comparable obstacle costs one additional exploration turn whenever it must be traversed. A durable fictional improvement can remove that recurring cost.

**Rationale:** Known routes should be faster than first exploration without becoming free or perfectly safe. Persistent terrain makes route choice and preparation matter during extraction, while the danger roll lets a conquered site change behind the party.

**Consequences:** Active light loses one turn for each travel turn and each persistent traversal cost. A room made dangerous by the travel roll remains conquered and cannot award its conquest Skill Tokens again. The GM or solo player introduces a context-appropriate new danger using the room, Domain, Theme, and current fiction. The three-room allowance and 1-in-6 chance require playtesting.

## 2026-03-31 — Make normal monster attacks automatic

**Status:** Accepted for initial playtesting

**Decision:** When the dungeon controls the turn, a monster that is fictionally able to attack may choose a plausible target and deal 1 damage without making an attack roll or calling for a defense check. Apply relevant protection, then return control to the adventurers.

If no monster can currently reach or attack an adventurer, it must use the dungeon action to close distance, overcome cover, block an exit, improve its position, or take another fictionally supported action. A coordinated group or swarm damages only one adventurer with a normal attack.

**Rationale:** The dungeon already earned one meaningful action through an adventurer's failed consequential check. Another routine roll would weaken that consequence, slow combat, and risk nested turn handoffs. Automatic damage makes positioning and preventive choices more important than reactive dice.

**Consequences:** Normal monster attacks cannot critically succeed or fail. Guard, resistance, armor, position, terrain, defensive preparation, and monster traits may prevent, redirect, or modify damage when applicable. Special attacks may override the defaults but should be telegraphed. Any reaction check required by a special dungeon action resolves only that action and never grants the dungeon another action.

## 2026-03-31 — Use one extra starting item without carrying capacity

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision:** Each adventurer begins with one additional useful item beyond their other character-creation gear. The player may choose it or roll it from a short expedition-item table. Adventurers can also take useful items found during play. Do not use inventory slots, weight totals, or a carrying-capacity statistic.

Consumables are removed after use; durable items remain until lost, broken, spent, or abandoned. Ordinary fiction still limits enormous, bulky, or awkward objects and may require free hands, assistance, or situational disadvantage.

**Rationale:** One extra item makes preparation and emergent problem solving part of every character without adding an inventory-management subsystem. Fictional limits handle exceptional objects more directly than universal capacity arithmetic.

**Consequences:** Candidate armor benefits that increased carrying capacity must be revised. Found items remain useful rewards even though inventory space is not itself a pressure. A short starting-item table still needs to be written.

## 2026-03-31 — Treat Wounds with single-use medkits

**Status:** Accepted for initial playtesting

**Decision:** A medkit is a single-use item. Spend one exploration turn and consume it to convert one Wound into its normal Scar. Treatment in sufficient safety requires no check. Treatment during immediate danger requires an appropriate check; failure leaves the Wound and medkit intact unless the fiction specifically ruins the supply, and gives the dungeon its normal action. A lone adventurer can treat their own Wound.

**Rationale:** Automatic safe treatment keeps the procedure quick, while its exploration-turn cost, finite supply, and permanent Scar preserve attrition. Requiring a check only under immediate pressure makes emergency treatment dangerous without making ordinary treatment unreliable.

**Consequences:** Active light loses one turn whenever treatment is attempted. Field Surgeon and Cure Wounds remain valuable because they can bypass supply, checks, or permanent consequences according to their individual rules. The medkit's single use must be tracked despite the absence of general carrying capacity.
