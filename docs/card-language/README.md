# Card language and lifecycle

This is the authoring standard for every card set. Cards describe game state and consequences; they do not prescribe a menu of acceptable solutions.

## Universal Challenge lifecycle

1. Reveal the Room and the front of its Challenge.
2. Read every immediately perceptible fact on the front. Players may ask ordinary clarifying questions without spending an action or making a check.
3. A player **commits** to an **approach** that could overcome, bypass, or accept the Challenge. Make a check only when the outcome is uncertain and failure matters.
4. Reveal the hidden **Response side** before resolving that uncertain check.
5. Apply its Revelation, Failure, and While Active rules.
6. When the Challenge durably stops applying to the party's use of the Room, **conquer the Room**. A temporary distraction, stealthy passage, or escape does not conquer it if the Challenge will apply again on return.
7. On conquest, every active adventurer gains one Skill Token and the Challenge immediately reveals its one guaranteed Quest or Discovery card, if any. Rest, search, and onward exploration become available.

Conquest has no bespoke card text. It always means that the Challenge card no longer applies. An **Ends When** field appears only when that boundary would otherwise be genuinely unclear.

## Scenes

A quiet aftermath, clue tableau, or other situation without active opposition is a **Scene**, not a Challenge. Its Room begins conquered, it has no hidden Response side, and it grants no Skill Token. It may immediately reveal the card named on the Scene and the Room remains eligible for its normal search.

## Card schemas

### Challenge

Front: Title; sensory, immediately perceptible situation; Immediate instruction; guaranteed reward icon, if any.

Response: **Revelation**, **Failure**, optional **While Active**, and **Ends When** only if the universal conquest boundary is ambiguous.

Do not print Examine, Engage, Possible Resolutions, or a bespoke Conquer field. Do not hide evidence that a person in the Room can already see, hear, smell, or feel.

### Room

A Room card contains the floorplan, architecture, lighting, visible fixed terrain, former function, tags, and read-aloud prose. It does not repeat universal search rules or reveal Quest, Challenge, or Monster information.

### Monster

A Monster card contains Signs, HP, Defense, Instinct, optional Special text, and its Wound. Signs announce the creature in the fiction. Instinct chooses targets and behavior. Do not repeat the universal dungeon-action procedure.

### Quest

A Quest card contains its Finding, Meaning or Use, and Claim instruction when it is a concrete element. Generation rolls, table bands, and debugging metadata never appear on a play-facing card.

### Discovery

**Discovery** is the broad reward-card type. **Loot** means only a portable Discovery. Subtypes are Item, Supply, Treasure, Evidence, Feature, and Route.

Use an exact handling verb:

- **Take:** add a portable card to an adventurer or the party.
- **Spend:** discard or mark a finite use.
- **Attach:** leave a Feature with its Room.
- **Map:** draw or establish a Route connection.
- **Record:** retain Evidence as a fact that can support later action.

### Gear and armor

Gear uses Attack, Passive, and Technique. Armor uses Protection and Burden. A Torch is a full Light reference card, not a one-line inventory label.

## Defined terms

- **Commit:** state the intended action and accept that its Response and check will now resolve.
- **Approach:** the fictional action and desired result, not an ability-button category.
- **Check:** a roll made only when outcome is uncertain and failure matters.
- **Room target:** the Room card's default check number.
- **Response side:** hidden consequences and rules revealed after commitment.
- **Active:** still able to affect the party's use of the Room.
- **Conquer:** make the primary Challenge durably stop applying.
- **Dungeon action:** one hostile action selected through the active creature's Instinct and the fiction.
- **Reveal:** turn a facedown card face up; this does not also claim it unless stated.
- **Claim:** take control of an already revealed reward or objective.
- **Permanently Lit:** Room actions do not spend portable Light there, darkness disadvantage is removed, and wandering monsters are suppressed.
- **Portable Light:** tokens on the active Torch card.

## Writing rules

- Put visible facts on the front and hidden consequences on the Response side.
- State triggers first: “When…”, “After…”, or “While…”.
- Use exact nouns and verbs. Avoid “eligible,” “qualifying,” “appropriate,” “may justify,” and undefined supernatural categories.
- Never list preferred solutions. State the obstacle, pressure, and consequences precisely enough for free-form solutions to be judged.
- Do not repeat universal rules on individual cards.
- Do not print random-generation metadata on play-facing cards.
- One Challenge reveals at most one guaranteed reward card. Searching is a separate optional gamble.
